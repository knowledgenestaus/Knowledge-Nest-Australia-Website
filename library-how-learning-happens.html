Memory 11

Working memory is where we think, and it can hold only a little at once. Long-term memory is where knowledge is stored, and it has no known limit. Learning is a lasting change in long-term memory, and students remember what they think about.

Prior knowledge and schema 8

What students already know shapes what they can learn next. New ideas stick when they connect to knowledge that is already organised in long-term memory, which is why thinking skills depend on knowing things. For curriculum that builds knowledge in order, see Knowledge-rich curriculum and coherence.

Novices and experts 10

Novices and experts learn differently. Beginners need full guidance and worked examples, and that guidance should fade as knowledge grows, because support that helps a novice can get in the way of someone more expert. Researchers call this the expertise reversal effect. For the teaching that follows from this, see Explicit instruction.
