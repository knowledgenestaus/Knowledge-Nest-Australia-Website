Learning difficulties, disabilities and inclusion

Learning difficulties, disabilities and inclusion

Learning difficulties and learning disabilities are not the same thing, and the difference matters for what a school does next. A learning difficulty is any struggle with reading, writing or maths, often the result of gaps in teaching, missed schooling or other circumstances, and it usually responds well to strong teaching and timely intervention. A learning disability, such as dyslexia, dysgraphia or dyscalculia, is neurodevelopmental and lasts. Good teaching and intervention still help a great deal, but the difficulty does not simply go away. I've gathered webinars, short videos, guides and research on both, alongside developmental language disorder, ADHD, autism and inclusive practice.

Practices in this area:Early identificationEvidence-based interventionReasonable adjustmentsIndividual Education PlansWorking with specialists and support staff

These resources are here to support your teaching. They are not a substitute for assessment or advice from a qualified professional, such as a psychologist, paediatrician or speech pathologist. Only a qualified professional can diagnose a learning disability. Support should never wait for a diagnosis, and a referral need not wait either. In July 2025 the American Psychiatric Association clarified that the DSM-5 does not require six months of specialised intervention on top of six months of quality schooling that targets the student's weakness.

Learning disabilities

Specific learning disorders that persist despite good teaching and intervention. They are usually diagnosed by a psychologist, who checks that the difficulty has lasted at least six months despite teaching aimed at it, and that it is not explained by gaps in instruction. They count as a disability under Australian law.

Persistent difficulty with accurate and fluent word reading and spelling, and the teaching and intervention that the evidence supports.
