Literacy

Frameworks and research behind literacy

Good literacy teaching rests on a handful of big ideas about how children learn to read, write and use language. I've gathered the frameworks and the research behind them here, so you can see where our practice comes from and explain it with confidence.

The Simple View of Reading 5

Reading comprehension is the product of decoding and language comprehension, so a reader needs both, and a weakness in either one limits understanding. Philip Gough and William Tunmer proposed it in 1986, and Wesley Hoover and Gough set it out more fully in 1990.

The Language House 5

Pamela Snow pictures language and literacy as a house. The solid ground is the warm, responsive relationships of a child's first two years, and the foundations are the oral language built across the first five. Two walls rise together and hold each other up, with reading, writing and spelling on one side and the language skills for getting along with others on the other. Social and emotional wellbeing and social cognition form the rafter, and the roof is a young person's way into further education, training and work.
