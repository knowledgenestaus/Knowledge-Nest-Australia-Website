Assessment

Assessment

Different assessments answer different questions. Start with the guide to free assessment tools, which says what each one is for, who it suits, how it is given and how long it takes. Below it are webinars, podcasts and articles on choosing assessments and using the results.

Practices in this area:ScreeningDiagnosticsProgress monitoringModerationReporting

These tools help you decide what to teach next and who needs more support. None of them diagnoses a learning disability, which takes a qualified professional such as a psychologist or speech pathologist.

Free assessment tools 24

Every tool here is free to use, some with a free sign-in. Australian tools come first. Each tool is described in the terms its publisher uses. Where the publisher does not state something, the card says so.

Screening
A quick check given to everyone, to find the students who may need more support. It tells you who, not why.
Diagnostic
A closer look at one student, usually after screening, to find out exactly what they can and cannot do so teaching can be targeted. It does not diagnose a disability.
Progress monitoring
Short, repeated checks for students receiving extra support, to see whether it is working.
Questionnaire
A set of questions a student, parent or teacher answers, about feelings or beliefs to do with reading.
Norms
A table of typical scores, so one student's result on another test can be compared with students of the same age.

DIBELS 8th Edition: Australasian version (opens in a new tab)

By University of Oregon

Assesses
Reading
Year levels
Foundation to Year 8
How it is given
One-to-one for five of the six measures. Maze can be given to a group.
Time
One minute each for five of the six measures. Maze takes three minutes.
What it measures and why you would use it
What it measures
A set of short fluency measures for assessing the acquisition of literacy skills, namely letter naming fluency, phonemic segmentation fluency, nonsense word fluency, word reading fluency, oral reading fluency and Maze. For dyslexia screening, the subtests measure processing speed, phonological awareness and the alphabetic principle.
Stated purpose
Universal screening, benchmark assessment and progress monitoring. The publisher describes it as a way to regularly detect risk and monitor the development of early literacy and early reading skills.

This is the version adapted for Australia and New Zealand. Use it in place of the American materials.

Free downloadOpens at dibels.uoregon.edu

ScreeningAustralian

Year 1 Phonics Check (opens in a new tab)

By Australian Government, on the Literacy Hub

Assesses
Phonics and decoding
Year levels
Year 1 (usually Term 3), Year 2 and older struggling readers
How it is given
One-to-one, online or on paper
Time
About 7 minutes per student
What it measures and why you would use it
What it measures
Students' decoding skills across words of increasing complexity, using real words and pseudowords. Students are categorised as fluent decoders (28 to 40), developing decoders (20 to 27) or struggling decoders (0 to 19).
Stated purpose
Identifying decoding strengths and instructional needs for individual students, classes and year levels, identifying students who might need phonics intervention, and tracking the effectiveness of phonics instruction over time.

Designed for students who have had at least 18 months of systematic synthetic phonics teaching.

Free with a teacher sign-inOpens at literacyhub.edu.au

By Australian Government, on the Mathematics Hub

Assesses
Early number
Year levels
Year 1
How it is given
One-to-one interview, online or on paper
Time
Not stated. Choose the 20-item check or the shorter 12-item check.
What it measures and why you would use it
What it measures
Whether students are developing the number and counting knowledge expected in Year 1. The 20-item check covers numeral identification, number sequences, skip counting, subitising, counting, addition and subtraction (including partitioning and flexible strategies) and multiplicative thinking.

DiagnosticAustralian

CC2: Castles and Coltheart Test 2 (opens in a new tab)

By MOTIf, Macquarie University (Anne Castles and colleagues)

Assesses
Single-word reading
Year levels
Ages 6 to 11 and a half (Australian norms)
How it is given
One-to-one
Time
Not stated. Up to 120 words, stopping after five errors in a row of each type.
What it measures and why you would use it
What it measures
The key processes in single-word reading, namely sounding-out ability, assessed by accuracy reading nonwords aloud, and whole-word recognition ability, assessed by accuracy reading irregular words aloud. It has 40 regular words, 40 irregular words and 40 nonwords, presented one at a time in mixed order with increasing difficulty.
Stated purpose
Comparing a child's accuracy on each item type with norms from over 1000 Australian children aged 6 years 0 months to 11 years 6 months (the online version converts scores to z scores).
