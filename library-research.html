How learning works 10

Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning (opens in a new tab)

By John Sweller · 1988 · Foundational research · Cognitive Science

The paper that launched cognitive load theory. Sweller shows that conventional problem solving can place such a heavy load on working memory that little is left over for learning.

Opens at doi.org

Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work (opens in a new tab)

By Kirschner, Sweller and Clark · 2006 · Foundational research

The landmark paper arguing that minimally guided approaches, such as discovery and inquiry learning, are less effective than guided instruction for novices.

Opens at caseresources.hsph.harvard.edu

Cognitive Architecture and Instructional Design: 20 Years Later (opens in a new tab)

By Sweller, van Merriënboer and Paas · 2019 · Open access · Educational Psychology Review

The authors of the landmark 1998 account of cognitive load theory review two decades of further research, including the effects teachers now rely on such as worked examples, split attention and expertise reversal.

FoundationalOpens at doi.org

Story of a Research Program (opens in a new tab)

John Sweller's own account of how cognitive load theory grew over four decades, from early problem-solving experiments to the theory used in classrooms today.

The NSW paper that describes the research on cognitive load theory and what it means for teaching.
