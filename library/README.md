# The Knowledge Nest Library

The library pages on the site are generated. Nobody edits `library*.html` by hand.

## How an update flows

1. **The catalogue sheet is the source.** Entries are added or corrected there.
2. **`library/data/library.json`** is the cleaned, publishable copy of the catalogue. It holds only
   entries whose link loaded and whose status is not "Needs checking" or "To source".
   It is produced by `library/tools/prepare.py` from an export of the sheet.
3. **`python3 library/build.py`** turns `library.json` into the library pages, the home page's
   library sections, the search index and the sitemap. The header, menu and footer are copied from
   `about.html`, so a menu change there flows to every library page on the next build.
4. **GitHub does the rebuild automatically.** Whenever a change under `library/` reaches the `main`
   branch, the "Library upkeep" workflow runs the build and commits the refreshed pages.
5. **Links are checked every week.** The same workflow runs `library/tools/check_links.py` each
   Monday morning and opens an issue listing any link that has died.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `library/build.py` | The page generator. Also holds the "Start here" topics, the must-listen shows and the categories. |
| `library/data/library.json` | Published entries, areas, shows and the Victorian view. |
| `library/data/summaries.json` | Short video summaries, keyed by link. |
| `library/data/shows.json` | Podcast show links, cover art addresses and descriptions. |
| `library/data/thumbs.json` | Preview picture addresses, keyed by an entry's link. Pictures stay on their creators' servers; nothing is copied here. Watch and Learn show them as the card's still, Read as a picture across the top of the card (the article's own lead image; without one the card shows the publication's name on pale blue, and `READ_PICTURE` in `build.py` switches back to the small side picture), Research and Curriculum as a small picture beside the heading. For a Substack post the address is the post's `cover_image` (or its og:image) on Substack's image server at 600 pixels wide; a publication's default share card, a picture used on more than two of its posts, an image under 400 pixels wide and anything that is an advertisement are left out. Episode cards use their show's cover art from `shows.json`. |
| `library/tools/prepare.py` | Sheet export to `library.json`. |
| `library/tools/check_links.py` | Link checker. |
| `library/data/featured.json` | This week's three featured Substack articles and the archive of earlier picks (see "This week's reading" below). |
| `library/tools/feature_week.py` | Sets the week's picks in `featured.json`, or suggests recent free posts to choose from. |
| `library/search.js`, `library/filter.js` | Search on the library home (titles, creators, areas and summaries), filters on Watch, Learn, Read and Listen. |

## Ground rules

- Link out; never copy or host other people's work.
- Every entry names its creator or source.
- The catalogue sheet's working notes stay out of this public repository.

## Added in the November 2026 redesign

- **Learn page** (`library-learn.html`): webinars, talks, school stories, interviews and short explainers. Add entries to `library/data/learn.json`. Entries with a `staff` list of prompts also appear under "Run it as a staff session". (The old `move` list has gone: see "Watch and Learn" below.)
- **Substacks** ("Substacks worth following" on `library-read.html`): edit `library/data/substacks.json`. Each has an `icon`, the publication's own Substack logo (its `logo_url`, on Substack's image server at 112 pixels square); with no icon the card shows the publication's initial. The cards are one list in the order of `SUBSTACK_ORDER` in `build.py` (Haili Hughes first, then the Australian voices, then the rest), and each names its region. A Substack not in that list goes last and the build prints a WARNING. To feature must-follows under their own heading first, add their titles to `MUST_SUBSTACKS`.
- **Illustrations**: drop a square `.webp` into `assets/illo/` named `watch`, `learn`, `listen`, `read`, `research`, `voice`, `curriculum`, `areas`, `home` or `notfound`, then run `python3 library/build.py`. Until a file exists, tiles show the number of resources instead.
- **Page not found** (`404.html`) is generated by the same script.
- The header motto and menu live in `about.html`; library pages copy their header and footer from it.

## Added in the post-launch polish (November 2026)

- **Public addresses have no `.html`.** The host serves `/library-watch` and the home page as `/`, so canonical links, share addresses, structured data and `sitemap.xml` use that form (`public()` in `build.py`). Links inside the pages keep their file names.
- **Build warnings.** `build.py` prints a `WARNING` line for a summary or description with a colon before a lower-case word (reword it, because the colon rule would put a capital mid-sentence), for a "Start here" pick that matches no title, and for an empty or "None" detail on a card.
- **Search** also looks in each entry's topic line and summary, finds American and Australian spellings of the same word, and offers results 60 at a time. Results with the whole phrase in the title come first, then those with every word in the title, then the rest.
- **Cache versions.** When `styles.css`, `search.js` or `filter.js` changes, raise its `?v=` number wherever it is referenced (`about.html` and the other hand-written pages for the stylesheet, `build.py` for the two scripts).

## Areas organised by strand (November 2026)

- **Learning difficulties, disorders and additional needs** (area 25, in MTSS and assessment) is laid out by strand, one series per topic, instead of by type. The strands, their one-sentence descriptions, the page introduction and the note under it live in `STRANDS` in `build.py`, keyed by area number.
- **To add an entry to a strand**, give it the area number in `areas` and a `strand` whose text matches a strand name exactly (or a list of two names when the entry sits in two strand areas). Put the area number first only when the entry is mainly about that area; the first area decides where a card sits on the Watch, Learn and Read shelves. The build prints a `WARNING` for an entry in the area with a missing or unknown strand.
- **Inside a strand** cards run Learn, Watch, Read, Research, Listen, each card shows its type, and sources are spread out within each type. An area that is not in `STRANDS` is laid out by type, as before.

## Watch and Learn, Literacy and Maths (reorganised November 2026)

- **Every video has a `kind`, and the kind decides its shelf.** Watch is only video of teachers teaching: `classroom footage`, `classroom footage with commentary` or `demonstration`. Everything else you sit down with is Learn: `webinar`, `talk`, `explainer`, `school story`, `interview` or `series`. It does not matter which file the entry is typed into (`library.json` with type `watch`, or `learn.json`). The build stops with a message if a video has no valid kind. The wording shown on the card (Lesson footage, Lesson with commentary, Demonstration, Webinar, Talk, Explainer, School story, Interview, Series) is `KIND_LABEL` in `build.py`.
- **A Learn entry needs `audience`** ("Teachers" or "Leaders") and should have `length`: "Under 10 minutes", "10 to 30 minutes", "30 to 60 minutes", "Over 60 minutes" or "Series". With no `length` it is filed under "Length not stated" in the Length menu, and the build lists those entries in a warning.
- **Menus.** Watch and Learn share two drop-down menus, "Topic" and then "Area of practice", which lists only the areas inside the chosen topic. They use the library's ten topics and their areas (`DOMAIN_ORDER` and `DOMAIN_OF`), and an option appears on a shelf only when that shelf has an entry for it. Listen keeps its own categories (`TOPICS`). A link such as `library-watch.html?domain=Literacy&areas=27` opens a shelf with the menus set.
- **Page layout.** Menus first, then the topic buttons, a short featured strip, a section for each topic with ten or more entries on the shelf (`TOPIC_SECTION_MIN`), then the full list. The three "Start with these" videos on Watch are `WATCH_START` in `build.py`.
- **Literacy has ten areas and Maths six.** They are ordinary areas of practice (numbers 26 to 39, with 10 Writing and 11 Oral language carried over), shown in the order written in `DOMAIN_OF`. An entry carries at most two Literacy areas (plus the frameworks area) or two Maths areas. The retired areas are 7, 8, 9 and 12: the build stops if an entry still uses one. "Maths" is the one name used for the topic.
- **Frameworks and research behind literacy** (area 33) is laid out by strand, like the learning difficulties area. Its strands and their descriptions are in `STRANDS[33]`; a strand with no entries is left out until it has some.
- **Retired addresses.** `RETIRED_PAGES` in `build.py` lists each old page and the page that replaced it. The build writes a small "This page has moved" page at the old address (not indexed, not in the sitemap or the search index). Nothing on the site links to them.

## Read shelf, Substack key articles and "This week's reading" (November 2026)

- **Read works like Watch and Learn.** It has the same "Topic" then "Area of practice" menus, topic buttons and topic sections,
  then the full list 24 cards at a time. Its third menu is "Publication or source": the Substacks first, then every other source.
  A Substack post is filed under its publication (matched by web address against `substacks.json`), so
  `library-read.html?by=Filling+the+Pail` opens the shelf showing only that Substack's key articles.
- **Key Substack articles** are ordinary Read entries in `library.json` with `meta` ["Substack", year, region] and the tag "Free".
  Only free posts are listed (Substack audience "everyone", no paywall). Credit them as "Author (Publication)", or the author
  alone where the publication is simply the author's name ("Tom Sherrington", "Haili Hughes", "Nathaniel Swain").
- **"Substacks worth following"** is its own section at the bottom of Read, reached from the "Jump to" links. A Substack card
  with key articles on the shelf shows "See key articles" and how many; the link sets the Publication menu.
- **Numbered series stay together.** Parts titled "... episode 3", "... Part 2", "... session 4" or "Title, #3: ..." are kept
  together in order (`_SERIES` in `build.py`). "Tip #49" or "Research Bite #56" with no comma before the "#" are separate pieces.

### This week's reading

Three free Substack articles, chosen each week and approved by Jo before they go live. They show as "This week's reading"
at the top of the home page's library block and at the top of Read.

`library/data/featured.json`:

```json
{"week_of": "2026-11-16",
 "intro": "optional line under the heading",
 "items": [{"url": "...", "title": "...", "by": "Author (Publication)", "why": "One or two sentences in Jo's voice.", "image": "lead image address (optional)"}],
 "archive": [{"url": "...", "title": "...", "by": "...", "week_of": "2026-11-09"}]}
```

- The panel is left out of the pages when the file is missing, empty, has no items, or its `week_of` is more than 21 days
  ago (`FEATURED_DAYS` in `build.py`; the build prints a WARNING). Because the pages are not rebuilt every day, a short script
  after the panel also hides it in the browser once the week is more than 21 days old, so a missed week never shows stale picks.
- For testing, `KN_FEATURED_FILE=/path/to/copy.json` builds from another copy of the file and `KN_TODAY=YYYY-MM-DD` stands in
  for today's date. Run those on a copy of the site, not the repository.

**Each week.** List candidates, choose three (different publications and topics, useful to a teacher this term), and send
them to Jo with the `why` lines. Once she approves:

```
python3 library/tools/feature_week.py --suggest              # free posts from the last 14 days (--days 21 to look further)
python3 library/tools/feature_week.py URL1 URL2 URL3 \
    --why "Why the first is worth reading." --why "..." --why "..." \
    [--title "" --title "Shorter card title" --title ""] [--week 2026-11-23] [--intro "..."] [--dry-run]
python3 library/build.py
```

- `--suggest` reads every Substack in `substacks.json`, pages through its archive (`/api/v1/archive?sort=new&limit=25`, then
  `&cursor=<nextCursor>`; the archive no longer accepts `offset` or `limit=50`) and prints free articles from the last 14 days
  that are not already in the library and not featured recently.
- Setting the week also records each post's lead image (`image`), by the same rules as `thumbs.json`, and prints it: look at each picture before Jo approves, because an advertisement is not caught automatically. A pick with no suitable image shows its publication's name instead.
- Setting the week reads each post's title and author from `<publication>/api/v1/posts/<slug>` (or the page's own tags),
  checks it is free (audience "everyone" and no "Keep reading with a 7-day free trial" or "This post is for paid subscribers"
  on the page), refuses any address featured in the last 12 weeks, moves the current picks into `archive`, writes
  `featured.json` and prints a summary. If any check fails nothing is written. It also flags a `why` line with a dash,
  a colon before a lower-case word or an American spelling.
- `--week` is the Monday the picks are for. Left out, it is today if today is Monday in Melbourne, otherwise next Monday.
- `--data DIR` points at a copy of `library/data` for a trial run. The script uses only Python's standard library; HTTPS goes
  through any proxy set in the environment.
