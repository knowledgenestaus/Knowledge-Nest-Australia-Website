Week of 16 November 2026

This week's reading

Three free pieces I think are worth your time this week, chosen from the Substacks I follow most closely. Each one opens on the writer's own Substack.

Minding the Gap

Yes, Writing Does Advance Thinking (opens in a new tab)

Natalie Wexler (Minding the Gap)

With end-of-year writing tasks everywhere, I loved Wexler's clear case for why writing about what students have learned deepens their thinking, in every subject and not just English.

Opens at nataliewexler.substack.com in a new tab

Tips for Teachers

Research Bite #77: Understanding the equals sign and solving equivalence problems (opens in a new tab)

Craig Barton (Tips for Teachers)

A quick, practical read for anyone teaching maths in the primary years. In this study nearly half of students finishing primary school read the equals sign as 'work out the answer', and those who saw it as meaning 'the same value' did better.

Opens at tipsforteachers.substack.com in a new tab

The Learning Dispatch

Is Understanding Just "Remembering in Disguise"? (opens in a new tab)

Carl Hendrick (The Learning Dispatch)

If you've ever heard memorisation and understanding set up as opposites, read this. Hendrick unpacks a new paper by John Sweller and colleagues arguing that understanding comes from remembering ideas that interact, which matters for how we plan explanations.

Opens at carlhendrick.substack.com in a new tab
